# Plan 9: Playarr on the TV

Drafted 2026-11-15 to scope it; not agreed yet (the decisions at the end come first).

**Goal:** the living-room TV plays Playarr from the sofa, with the remote: Made for you, the library and search on a ten-foot screen, the music going on with the screen off or the screensaver up, DJ transitions and even volume as on the phone, and the Ambilight lit from the cover playing. First for the owner's TV; then anyone's Android TV, from Google Play.

## The TV

**What this means for the design:**
- **Memory is the constraint.** Google's ceiling for low-RAM TVs is the target whatever the flag says: under 200 MB for the app's memory and graphics, under 280 MB in all. When the TV needs memory, a background app is what goes, and music is often the app in the background.
- **The CPU is weak.** Nothing is decoded or mixed on the TV that the server can do: no ALAC and no 24-bit FLAC, no mixing, no analysis.
- **It's an OLED.** A Now Playing screen left up for an album's length burns in. Nothing bright stays still.

## Decisions taken in this draft (open to change)

- **Native, in Kotlin: Jetpack Compose for TV (`androidx.tv:tv-material`) and Media3 (ExoPlayer in a `MediaSessionService`).**
  - **Not the PWA in a WebView.** A Trusted Web Activity needs Chrome, which Android TV doesn't have. A WebView adds a renderer process (about 55 MB after light use, by Google's figures). Its audio is tied to the page, so it can't keep playing in the background or give the Now Playing card that Google Play's TV rules ask for (TV-BA, TV-NP).
  - **Not Leanback.** Google has deprecated it in favour of Compose for TV.
  - **MediaSession** gives the remote's media keys, the system's Now Playing card, background playback and, later, Google Assistant's "play …", all at once.
  - This is the same call iOS made ([decisions.md](../decisions.md), 2026-11-03: native over a wrapper).
- **The server does the audio work.** The TV asks for the leveled AAC stream (`/stream/:id/levelled.aac?kbps=256&gain=`) by default. That one stream gives it even volume, no ALAC or hi-res decoding, little memory and little CPU. Original files are an option for FLAC on the home network. DJ transitions are the server's rendered files (16-bit FLAC, which Android decodes) played between the songs, as on iOS.
- **Signing in with a code shown on the TV.** Nobody types a password with a D-pad. The TV shows a short code and a QR code for `<server>/link?code=…`. Someone signed in on their phone or the web approves it there, choosing which account the TV gets, and the TV picks up a token of its own (session platform `tv`). It's the device-code flow every TV app uses, and it's new on the server.
- **In the monorepo as `apps/tv`**, outside the pnpm workspace like `apps/ios`. It's a Gradle project with three modules, mirroring iOS's packages:
  - `api`: the models, generated from `packages/shared` as the Swift ones are, the client, Bonjour (`NsdManager`) and tokens in the Android Keystore;
  - `player`: Media3, the queue, levels, transitions and play reports;
  - `app`: the screens.
  - Dependencies point one way: `app` on `player`, and both on `api`.
- **Package id `me.tracklistme.playarr.tv`**, so a phone app can later be `me.tracklistme.playarr` beside it.

## 1. Tools on this Mac and a first install — S

This Mac has no Java and no Android SDK, and Homebrew isn't writable (as with XcodeGen, [decisions.md](../decisions.md) 2026-11-03).

- Pin a JDK (Temurin 21) and Android's command-line tools, with checksums, under `apps/tv/.tools/`, fetched by `make -C apps/tv bootstrap`.
- Use the Gradle wrapper, checked in, and a Makefile with every entry point, as iOS has:
  - `make build`, `test`, `install` (adb to `TV_ADDRESS`, default the owner's TV), `logs`, `meminfo`, `screens`;
  - root scripts `pnpm tv:*`.
- Set up an Android TV emulator image (arm64), configured with 2 GB, for work away from the TV.
- **Done when** a "hello" app with a banner shows on the TV's home screen after `pnpm tv:install`, and `pnpm tv:meminfo` prints its memory. The `adb` readings in the table above are filled in.

## 2. Talking to the server — M

- **Kotlin models from `packages/shared`.** Teach `apps/ios/scripts/gen-models.mjs` a second output, or split out its JSON-Schema step and add a Kotlin writer. The models are `@Serializable` data classes (kotlinx.serialization), and string unions become enums that keep values they don't know, as the Swift rules do. `make models-check` covers both, and `ios-contracts.yml` becomes `contracts.yml`.
- **Contract tests.** Decode the same recorded fixtures as `ContractTests.swift`. Move them from `PlayarrAPI/Tests/…/Fixtures` to a shared `packages/shared/fixtures/`, or read them where they are.
- **The client.** `X-Playarr: 1` and `Authorization: Bearer` on every request, OkHttp with a small disk cache, and the same handling of 401 and offline as iOS.
- **Finding servers.** Bonjour `_playarr._tcp` through `NsdManager`, or an address typed with the remote. Plain HTTP only for local addresses, as iOS's `ServerAddress` allows.
- **The token never goes to an address the server hasn't proven.** Use the home-address proof from plan 1 §3 (`GET /api/auth/proof`) wherever the TV knows two addresses.
- **The server knows TVs.** `/api/server` answers `minTvVersion` beside `minAppVersion`, and Settings → Accounts → devices shows a TV as one.
- **Done when** the TV lists the albums of the NAS through the generated models, and the contract tests pass in CI.

## 3. Signing in from the sofa — S–M (server and both apps)

- **Server:**
  - `POST /api/auth/device` (open, rate-limited per address) answers `{ code, verifyUrl, pollToken, expiresIn }`. The code is 8 characters from the setup codes' alphabet and lasts 10 minutes.
  - `POST /api/auth/device/poll` with `pollToken` answers `pending`, `{ token, account }` once approved, or `expired`. It's polled every 5 s at most.
  - `POST /api/auth/device/approve` with `{ code }` (session) gives the device a session of the caller's account, named by the device.
  - Stored as SHA-256, like every code. Approving needs the approver to be signed in, so a code can't be used to break in.
- **Web:** a `/link` page. Signed in, it shows the code from the address and "Sign in the TV as <account>", with the accounts on this browser to choose from. Signed out, it signs in first. iOS gets the same page through the `playarr://link` link.
- **TV:**
  - Before signing in, it shows the code and its QR code, large.
  - In setup mode (no owner yet), it says to set up the server on a phone or computer first. Claiming a server from a D-pad isn't worth building.
  - Several accounts, switched from the side menu, as on iOS.
- **Done when** the TV signs in by scanning its QR code with the iPhone, a code that's expired or used is refused, and the session shows as a TV under the account's devices.

## 4. Playback — M–L

- **The service.** `MediaSessionService` with ExoPlayer: the queue, play, pause, next and previous from the remote's keys and the TV's media controls, and the system Now Playing card. It plays on after Home, under the screensaver and with the screen off, and it never stops Ambient Mode (Play's TV-BA).
- **Streams.**
  - `levelled.aac` at 256 kbps by default, with gains from `POST /api/levels`.
  - Settings → Playback → Quality: Original for files the TV can decode (FLAC up to 16/48, MP3, AAC; anything else stays encoded).
  - On a 503 with `Retry-After` (too many encodes), wait and try again, as iOS does.
- **Gapless.** The AAC streams carry the encoder's priming and padding in `iTunSMPB` (`apps/server/src/media/mp4.ts`). ExoPlayer trims `iTunSMPB` in plain MP4, but whether it does in **fragmented** MP4 is unknown.
  - **Spike first.** Play an album known to be gapless, across a join, and record the TV's output.
  - If ExoPlayer doesn't trim, the client clips each item by the priming and padding it can read from the stream's header (`ClippingMediaSource`), or the server adds the gapless info ExoPlayer reads.
- **DJ transitions.**
  - Ask for each pair through `POST /api/transitions`. Play A clipped at the transition's start, then the transition file, then B from the transition's end, all as one playlist of clipped items.
  - The planner in `packages/mix` is deterministic, so the times are the server's. The TV needs no planner of its own, only to read the times the transition answers.
  - A transition that isn't ready in time falls back to a plain change, as on iOS.
- **The rest.**
  - Play reports to `POST /api/plays`, with "this device counts towards history" (default on).
  - Radio when the queue ends, and the sleep timer.
  - Riding out a dead spot as plan 2 §5 did: retry at the time it stopped, with backoff.
- **No downloads.** The TV is always online, and its storage is small. A short cache of the songs up next is enough (Media3's `SimpleCache`, 200 MB).
- **Done when:**
  - an album plays gapless, a DJ mix joins on the beat, and even volume matches the iPhone's;
  - the music plays on with the screensaver up and after Home;
  - the remote's media keys work from any screen.

## 5. The ten-foot app — L

The screens, D-pad first (focus that's always visible and never lost, Back that always goes up), in the design language of [design.md](../design.md): the same palette, Gloock, Schibsted Grotesk and DM Mono, sized for three metres (the type table gets a TV column). **Navigation:** a side rail with Home, Search, Library and Settings, and the account at the top.

- **Home:** Jump back in, Made for you mixes, recently added. Covers are cards that grow on focus.
- **Library:** albums (paged from `/api/albums?offset=`), artists, playlists, favorites.
- **Album, artist, playlist and mix pages:** led by Play and Shuffle, as plan 6 §4 made them.
- **Search:** the TV's on-screen keyboard, results as the web shows them. Voice search through the system's `SpeechRecognizer` where the remote has a microphone.
- **Now Playing:** the cover large, with synced lyrics and Up next. The accent follows the cover (`coverAccent` from the API).
  - **OLED care.** After a minute without a key, it goes to an art mode: the cover drifts slowly, text dims, and nothing white stands still. It turns black at the TV's own screensaver.
  - This screen is the one most often left up, so it's the one measured for memory and for burn-in.
- **Settings:** quality, transitions (Off, Crossfade, DJ mix), history on this device, Ambilight, accounts, servers, about. Admin settings stay on the phone and the web.
- **Not on the TV:** requests and Discover, editing playlists, organizing sources, Settings → Updates, Sources and Accounts. A TV is shared, and these belong to a person's own device.
- **Images are within the budget.** Ask `/art` for the size each card shows. Use Coil with a memory cache capped at 32 MB and no full-size bitmaps.
- **Done when:**
  - every screen works with the remote alone;
  - an hour of browsing and playing stays under 200 MB (`dumpsys meminfo`), with no jank on Home's rows on this TV;
  - the app comes back to the same page after the TV reclaims it in the background.

## 6. The Ambilight follows the music — M

- **The colours.**
  - From the cover playing: `coverColor` and `coverAccent`, which the API already gives, or a few colours sampled at the cover's edges, one per side, sent per side.
  - They change with the song, faded over the transition.
  - Optionally they pulse on the beat from the song's mix map (`POST /api/mix-maps`), as the iOS app keeps time with the music (plan 7 §2).
- **How.** JointSpace from the app to the TV itself: pair once on the TV (`/6/pair/request`, the PIN on screen, `/6/pair/grant`) and keep the credentials in the Keystore. Then `ambilight/mode` is set to `manual` and colours go to `ambilight/cached`, and the user's own mode is put back on stop.
- **Behind a switch, off by default.** If the API doesn't answer (another firmware, or another brand of TV), the switch says so and the app carries on.
- **Spike first:** whether an app on the TV can reach its own JointSpace port (127.0.0.1 or its LAN address), and whether 1925 answers without pairing on this firmware.
- **Done when** the lights take the cover's colours within a second of a song starting and give back the TV's own mode when Playarr stops.

## 7. Building, releasing and updating — M

- **CI:** `.github/workflows/tv.yml`, on changes to `apps/tv/**`, `packages/shared/**` or the workflow.
  - It runs `make -C apps/tv test` (unit tests, the contract tests and Android Lint) on Ubuntu with the pinned JDK.
  - It builds a release APK and an app bundle, signed with the upload key from secrets.
  - Version code: the commit count, as iOS's build number. Version name: `1.x`, kept by hand like the iOS app's.
- **The owner's TV:**
  - `pnpm tv:install` for development.
  - For releases, the app updates itself from GitHub. On launch and daily, it checks the repository's newest release for a `playarr-tv-<version>.apk` asset, and Settings → About offers "Install update". Android asks to confirm, and the app needs "install unknown apps" allowed once.
  - Releases come from CI on a `tv-v*` tag, so they follow the server's channels: tags for stable.
  - Sideloaded apps are exempt from Google's developer verification, so the TV needs no store at all.
- **Google Play**, once the app is good enough for others:
  - A developer account ($25), Play App Signing, and the internal testing track first.
  - A personal account opened after November 2023 needs a closed test with 12 testers for 14 days before production. An organization account doesn't.
  - Play's TV requirements:
    - `CATEGORY_LEANBACK_LAUNCHER` and a 320×180 banner;
    - touchscreen declared as not required, and full D-pad use;
    - a Now Playing card, and Ambient Mode left alone;
    - an app bundle with 64-bit and 16 KB page-size support (required since August 2026);
    - a privacy policy (the one the iOS listing has).
  - The listing lives in the repo as iOS's does (`apps/tv/store/`), uploaded through the Play Developer API with a service account in secrets.
- **The server and old apps:** `minTvVersion` follows the rule in [ios/release.md](../ios/release.md) for raising `minAppVersion`.
- **Done when** a tag builds a signed APK on GitHub, the TV offers it and installs it from Settings, and (for Play) an internal-testing build reaches the TV through the Play Store.

## Order

1 → 2 → 3 → the gapless and Ambilight spikes → 4 → 5 → 7 (sideloaded updates) → 6 → 7 (Play) → 8 as it goes.

The first usable build (sign in, browse, play with even volume and transitions, the Now Playing card) is the end of 5. The Ambilight comes after because it's the delight, not the player.

## Risks

- **Memory:** a TV that reclaims the app mid-song stops the music. The service holds a foreground notification while playing, and the screens are kept light (step 5's budget).
- **Gapless in fragmented MP4** (step 4's spike): if neither fix works, albums on the TV play from the original files (FLAC), and only leveled songs that aren't album-ordered get AAC.
- **JointSpace is unofficial:** firmware updates have broken it before. It stays a switch that can fail quietly.
- **Firmware:** a TP Vision update bricked many TPM191E sets in 2025. Nothing in the plan needs a newer firmware, so don't update the TV for this work.
