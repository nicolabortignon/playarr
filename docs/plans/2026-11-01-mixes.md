# Made for you: generated, personal mixes

Playlists Playarr builds by itself from what's in the library and how it's listened to. They sit at the
top of Home, open like playlists, can be saved as real playlists, and change on their own schedule.

## Listening history (the foundation)

Playarr didn't record listening. Now the player reports each song when it stops being the current one:

- **played**: heard for at least 30 s, or half of a shorter song
- **skipped**: left for another song after 3–30 s (under 3 s is just browsing, not a verdict)

Each report carries the local hour and weekday (the server's clock may be in another time zone), how long
it was heard, and the song. Reports that can't be sent wait on the device and go with the next one.

Table `plays`: recording, track, artist, album, played_at, hour, weekday, seconds, skipped.
Settings → Mixes can turn recording off and clear the history.

## Signals

- **Artist affinity**: plays (half-life 60 days), favorites (+3 per song), playlist songs (+1), skips (−0.5)
- **Song affinity**: the same per song
- **Style**: ListenBrainz tags → style families (analysis/styles.ts), else the artist's genres
- **Sound**: measured audio features where analyzed (energy from loudness, busyness, pulse, tempo),
  else an estimate from tags (calm tags, families)
- **Era**: album year, else the ListenBrainz first release year
- **When**: album added_at; release dates for "on this day"

Tags are fetched for the whole library in the background (cheap: 25 songs per ListenBrainz call). Measuring
the sound of the whole library is heavy, so it's a setting (off by default); mixes work without it.

## The mixes

| Mix | What | Changes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Vibes of the hour | What you play around this hour (±1 h, same kind of day weighted up): its artists, styles and energy; half familiar, half like it. Without history: a time-of-day default (calm mornings, upbeat afternoons, warm evenings, quiet nights). | hourly |
| Discovery Weekly | Songs in your library you've never played, from artists and styles you like, plus ListenBrainz neighbours of your most played songs. "Adventurous" setting: from more of what you like to further afield. | Mondays |
| On Repeat | Your most played songs of the last 30 days. | daily |
| Blast from the past | Songs you played a lot, not heard for a while (setting: 30–365 days). Without history: favorites, then albums from the library's early days. | daily |
| Deep cuts | The less played songs of your favourite artists. | daily |
| Fresh arrivals | Songs from albums added in the last 30 days that you haven't played, a few per album. | daily |
| Time machine | One mix per decade you have the most of, e.g. "The 90s". | daily |
| Genre mixes | One per style you listen to most, e.g. "Electronic mix". | daily |
| Energy boost | Loud, busy, steady beat, building up. | daily |
| Wind down | Calm, soft, slow, settling down. | daily |
| Deep focus | Instrumental-leaning, steady, mid-to-low energy (ambient, classical, post-rock, jazz…). | daily |
| On this day | Songs from albums released this week in past years. | daily |

Each mix: deterministic for its period (seeded by mix and period, so it doesn't reshuffle on every visit),
at most 2–3 songs per artist, no artist twice in a row, and hidden when it has under 8 songs. "New mix"
draws another one for the same period. Generated mixes are stored, so they survive restarts.

## Settings → Mixes (for everyone)

- Show each kind of mix (switches)
- Songs per mix: 25 / 50 / 75 / 100
- Discovery: familiar ↔ adventurous
- Blast from the past: not heard for 30 / 90 / 180 / 365 days
- How many genre mixes and decade mixes (0–4)
- Remember what's played (switch) and clear the history
- Measure the sound of the whole library (switch)

## API

- `POST /api/plays` `{ plays: PlayReport[] }`
- `GET /api/mixes` → `MixSummary[]`; `GET /api/mixes/:key` → `MixDetail`
- `POST /api/mixes/:key/regenerate`; `POST /api/mixes/:key/save` → a playlist
- `GET/PUT /api/settings/mixes`, `DELETE /api/settings/mixes/history` (home network only)

## Covers

Drawn in the browser, no server rendering: up to four album covers of the mix in a grid, under a colour
wash per kind of mix, with the mix's name. Cheap on a NAS, and recognisable.
