# Plan 8: Mood mixes that mean what they say

Written 2026-11-07, after Energy boost and Wind down were found "not very good". Playarr now measures far more of every song than these mixes used. This plan has two parts: what each mood mix is for and what defines it (the product spec), then how Playarr matches songs to it from what it knows (the algorithm). It was built the same day (where it stands, below).

**Goal:** a mood mix plays as if someone who knows the music made it for that moment. Every song belongs, the mix holds together, and it goes somewhere.

## Where it stands (2026-11-07)

- **Built on the server:**
  - `apps/server/src/mixes/mood.ts`: each song's qualities, and placing them within the library.
  - `generators.ts`: the four mood mixes and the rules for every mix.
  - `mixes.ts`: reads phrase energy and lyrics, and remakes the mixes once with the new recipe.
- **Chill is new**, in Settings → Mixes on the web and on iOS.
- **Tests:** a library of archetypes (`moodMixes.test.ts`) holds each mix to this spec, including a library measured only in part, minor keys with stray mood tags, a live album titled plainly and a slow swell. The qualities have tests of their own (`mood.test.ts`).
- **Reviewed** by a second pair of eyes the same day: a same-day remake leaned away from itself, a partly measured library could fill Wind down and Chill with dance-pop, Chill's mood was decided by noise, slow swells slipped through, live albums weren't seen as live, and short songs were dropped everywhere. All fixed, below.
- **Checked against real tags:** the tags ListenBrainz has for 20 albums (Kind of Blue, Rumours, Mezzanine, Moon Safari, Khruangbin…) come out as they should: Kind of Blue and Khruangbin without singing, Rumours sung and not calm, Kendrick Lamar's interlude a sketch.
- **Not yet:** checked against a real library's measured sound. The thresholds below are first choices, set against the archetypes. The next step is listening to them on the household's own music, then tuning.

## Why the old ones fell short

- **Energy boost:** mostly loudness. But loudness is mastering: a 2015 ballad is mastered louder than a 1977 punk single, so ballads made Energy boost.
- **Wind down:** anything quiet, which let in old rock that only sounds quiet next to today's masters. It also took dark ambient, horror scores, skits, live tracks with applause, and symphonies whose quiet average hides loud climaxes.
- **Deep focus:** went by genre. Vocal jazz and trip hop counted as music to focus to, and so did a soundtrack full of crescendos.
- **No coherence:** a mix could go from Chopin to drum and bass if both scored.
- **One shape:** songs sorted by energy in fixed steps of 0.15. A mix whose songs all fell within one step had no shape at all.
- **No rules against the never-right:** nothing ruled out intros, interludes, Christmas songs in July, or comedy.

## What Playarr knows about a song now

| What | From | Tells |
|---|---|---|
| Loudness, busyness (onsets a second), brightness (spectral centroid), bass share, loudness range, a 48-point loudness envelope | sound analysis (`dsp.ts`) | how hard and how full it sounds |
| Tempo, how sure the beat is, whether the grid holds | beat analysis (the mix map) | drive, groove |
| Key (Camelot) and how sure | the mix map | major or minor, a hint of mood |
| Phrase energy: each 16-beat phrase's level next to the loudest | the mix map | its shape: steady, building, quiet verses, a drop |
| Genre and mood tags, weighted (the recording's, then its album's, then its artist's) | ListenBrainz | style, and words like *mellow*, *dark*, *energetic* |
| Lyrics, or that it's instrumental | the song's file, or LRCLIB, for songs whose lyrics were opened | whether anyone sings |
| Title, length, year | the catalog | intros and skits, live recordings, Christmas songs, the era it was mastered in |
| Plays, skips, favorites, playlists | the account's history | what this listener likes |

## Product spec: what defines each mood mix

Each mood mix is a moment, not a genre. It is defined by how it should feel, what never belongs in it, and the shape it takes from the first song to the last.

### Energy boost: for moving

- **For:** a workout, cleaning the house, a commute you want to own, waking up a party.
- **Feels:** a driving beat, fast or forceful, a full sound that rarely lets up; uplifting more than bleak. Loud for its time, not just loudly mastered: 1965 garage rock belongs as much as 2015 house.
- **Never:** a ballad with one big chorus, a slow build that only arrives at the end, anything calm.
- **Shape:** starts strong, builds step by step, and finishes at the peak.
- **Words:** "A driving beat, building up". On the mix's page: "Your most driving songs, mostly electronic and hip hop today, 118–150 BPM, building to the peak: …".

### Chill: for an easy afternoon (new)

- **For:** a slow Sunday, cooking, friends over, reading in the sun.
- **Feels:** laid back but alive. An easy groove or a gentle sway, warm rather than bright, unhurried; it may have singing. Lo-fi beats, bossa nova, downtempo, neo-soul, mellow indie.
- **Never:** anything intense, dark or eerie, or heavy-hearted. No songs that swell to a big chorus, no applause, nothing so still it's sleepy (that's Wind down).
- **Shape:** level, rolling gently up and back down rather than going anywhere.
- **Words:** "Easy, warm, unhurried". On the mix's page: "Laid-back songs with an easy groove, warm and unhurried: …".

### Deep focus: for working

- **For:** work, study, reading, writing code.
- **Feels:** no words to follow, a steady pulse or a steady wash, nothing that jumps out. Present enough to keep you going, never asking for attention. Ambient, piano, post-classical, minimal techno, lo-fi beats.
- **Never:** singing, crescendos, sudden drops, tension, applause, very short pieces that keep breaking the flow.
- **Shape:** level, one long plateau, each song blending into the next.
- **Words:** "Steady and instrumental" ("Steady, mostly instrumental" when some songs may have singing).

### Wind down: for the end of the day

- **For:** the last hour before sleep, a bath, a slow train home.
- **Feels:** soft and slow, quiet next to music of its time, warm in tone. Gentle ballads, piano, ambient, quiet jazz.
- **Never:** anything sudden (a surge, a drop) or tense (dark, eerie, aggressive). No groove that wants you up, no applause, no interludes.
- **Shape:** settles as it goes, ending close to stillness.
- **Words:** "Soft and slow, settling down".

### Rules for every mix

- **Not songs:** short intros, outros, interludes and skits are never picked, nor spoken word, stand-up, audiobooks, podcasts or white noise. Anything under 30 s is never picked, and under a minute only with a sure beat (grindcore and punk, not a skit spoken over nothing). On Repeat shows what's played most, short songs included, but never a skit.
- **Christmas songs** only from 24 November to 6 January, in every mix, radio and Feeling lucky; On Repeat shows what was played.
- **Holding together:** a mood mix leans to a style or two of the day ("mostly electronic and hip hop today"), so it doesn't lurch between Chopin and drum and bass. The styles change from day to day.
- **Not yesterday's again:** a mix made again for a new day (or a new draw) leans away from the songs of the one it replaces.
- **What the listener likes** counts, but a mood mix is the mood first: liking can't carry a song that doesn't belong.
- **Belonging is absolute as well as relative:** a library measured only where it's loud still can't put dance-pop in Wind down, however soft it is next to the rest.
- At most two songs by one artist, and never the same artist twice in a row where it can be helped (as before).

## Algorithm

### 1. A song's qualities (`mood.ts`, `taste.ts`)

Every measurement is read as where the song stands in this library (its percentile among the measured songs). That way the mixes fit any library: Energy boost is the most intense part of a classical collection and of a metal collection alike.

- **Loudness is judged next to music of its time.** That means its percentile among songs released within 4 years of it, widened until there are at least 40, blended 60/40 with its percentile in the whole library.
- **Intensity**, 0–1, is built from five parts, each a percentile except the beat:

  | Part | Weight |
  |---|---|
  | Loudness, next to its time | 0.3 |
  | Busyness | 0.25 |
  | How sure the beat is | 0.15 |
  | Tempo: (BPM − 70) / 100, counted only as far as there's a beat | 0.15 |
  | Brightness | 0.15 |

  - A part that wasn't measured (the beat, before beat analysis) is left out, and the rest share its weight.
  - Tags move the result: energetic tags up to +0.08, calm ones down to −0.1.
  - The result is placed as a percentile again, and becomes the song's `energy`. Vibes of the hour, radio, Feeling lucky and blending use it too.
  - Its shape is deliberately not part of intensity: a quiet drone is as steady as a wall of noise.
- **Shape**, from phrase energy (else the loudness envelope), leaving out the first and last phrases:
  - *sustain* is the share of phrases within 6 dB of the loudest (a verse under its chorus counts, a breakdown doesn't);
  - *swing* is the standard deviation of the phrases' levels, in dB;
  - *surge* is the biggest rise from one phrase to the next, in dB.
- **Steadiness:** 1 − (swing − 1.5 dB) / 5 dB, kept between 0 and 1.
- **Absolute intensity:** the same five parts on analysis's own scales, the same in every library, kept beside the percentile. It guards each mix against the obviously wrong.
- **Mood words** are matched as whole words or phrases ("intense" isn't "tense", "funk" isn't "fun"), and each counts by its share of all the tag weight, so a stray tag barely moves anything:
  - *calm*: mellow, calm, relaxing, peaceful, chill, quiet, gentle, soothing, laid back… and soft, but not the genre "soft rock" (4 × its share, capped at 1);
  - *warmth*: 3 × (the share that's happy, uplifting, sunny, fun… minus the share that's sad, melancholic, dark, bleak…), between −1 and 1.
- **Mood** (bleak 0 to bright 1): 0.5 + 0.3 × warmth + 0.1 × (brightness − 0.5), plus or minus 0.05 for a major or minor key (scaled by how sure the key is). The key counts little: key detection is sure of a key against the keys that clash with it, not against its relative major or minor.
- **Tension:** 3 × the share of the tags that are dark, eerie, ominous, horror, aggressive, harsh, noise or industrial, plus 0.4 × its metal share and 0.25 × its experimental share. Capped at 1.
- **Singing** (vocals, the likelihood someone sings or raps), from the first source that knows:
  1. Lyrics found mean yes. LRCLIB's instrumental flag, lyrics that only say "[Instrumental]", or "Instrumental" in the title, mean no.
  2. Its tags, each taking its most specific hint: "vocal jazz" 0.95 before "jazz" 0.45, "post-rock" 0.12 before "rock" 0.85, "lo-fi hip hop" 0.2 before "hip hop" 0.9.
     - Tags that decide count for more than tags that say little either way: each weighs its weight × (0.2 + 1.6 × |hint − 0.5|), so "jazz" or "electronic" barely count against "bebop" or "vocal jazz".
     - A bare "instrumental" tag at least 3% of the strongest holds singing to 0.15 (listeners tag Kind of Blue so, lightly). "Instrumental rock" doesn't: crowds put it on Rumours too.
  3. Its style families: classical 0.15 up to hip hop 0.95.
  4. Otherwise 0.6.
- **Groove:** how sure the beat is × 0.75 if the grid wanders × how much the tempo sways (1 from 85 to 130 BPM, at half or double time) × (0.6 + 0.4 × bass).
- **Traits:**
  - *sketch*: an intro, outro, interlude or skit by its title, when under 150 s (M83's five-minute "Intro" is a song); a release whose type is Spokenword, Audiobook, Interview or Audio drama; or spoken word, stand-up, audiobook, podcast or white noise among its strongest tags (at least 0.9 of the strongest, so an artist who sometimes speaks over the music still makes songs). Not "comedy": Weird Al makes songs;
  - *live*: "(Live…", "[Live]" or "- Live" in the title, a release whose type is Live (live albums' songs aren't titled so), or a telling "live" tag (at least half the strongest). Not "Live Forever" or "Live in the Moment";
  - *Christmas*: by title (Christmas, Christmastime, Xmas, Santa Baby, Little Drummer Boy, Carol of the Bells, Joy to the World…), by album title (Christmas, Xmas, Navidad, Noël, Weihnachten), or a Christmas tag at least 0.6 of the strongest, so an artist with one Christmas album isn't a Christmas artist.
- **Too short:** under 30 s, or under a minute without a sure beat (0.5). A length not known yet (0) is never short.
- **Songs whose sound isn't measured** keep the old estimate from their styles (metal 0.88 down to classical 0.2, minus a little for calm tags), and count as unmeasured.

### 2. Who belongs, who comes first (`generators.ts`)

Measured songs that belong and songs that belong by their tags alone compete together, the guesses a little behind. Each song scores:

`0.5 × fit + 0.3 × liking + 0.12 × lanes + 0.1 × chance − 0.2 (known only by tags) − 0.3 (skipped more than played) − 0.12 (in the mix this one replaces)`

The best are taken, at most two per artist.

| Mix | Belongs (measured) | Belongs (tags only) | Fit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Energy boost | energy ≥ 0.7; absolute ≥ 0.5; sustain ≥ 0.6; not calm (calm tags < 0.5) | estimated energy ≥ 0.62, calm < 0.3 | 0.6 energy + 0.25 groove + 0.15 mood |
| Chill | 90 s or longer, not live; energy 0.15–0.6; absolute ≤ 0.65; tension < 0.4; mood ≥ 0.35; surge ≤ 5 dB; steadiness ≥ 0.3 (no slow swell) | calm tags ≥ 0.4 or ambient/downtempo style ≥ 0.4; tension < 0.3 | 0.25 near energy 0.38 (± 0.25) + 0.3 groove + 0.1 steadiness + 0.1 (1 − brightness) + 0.15 mood + 0.1 calm |
| Deep focus | 120 s or longer, not live; vocals ≤ 0.35; steadiness ≥ 0.5; energy 0.05–0.7; absolute ≤ 0.8; tension < 0.5; surge ≤ 8 dB | vocals ≤ 0.3, tension < 0.4 | 0.4 (1 − vocals) + 0.35 steadiness + 0.25 near energy 0.4 (± 0.4) |
| Wind down | 100 s or longer, not live; energy ≤ 0.3; absolute ≤ 0.45; groove ≤ 0.6; tension < 0.3; surge ≤ 7 dB; steadiness ≥ 0.3 | calm tags ≥ 0.5 or estimated energy ≤ 0.3; tension < 0.2 | 0.45 (1 − energy) + 0.2 steadiness + 0.15 (1 − brightness) + 0.1 (1 − groove) + 0.1 calm |

Unknown steadiness counts as 0.5, unknown groove as 0.3 in a fit (and as none against Wind down's limit), and an unknown surge as none.

### 3. Lanes: holding together

When at least 1.5 times as many songs belong as the mix holds, it picks the day's lanes:
- **The draw:** one style family, drawn by chance among the four strongest; a second the same way, if one is at least half as strong as the first. Each family is weighted by its share of each song that belongs × (0.3 + liking).
- **The floor:** a family needs at least 15% of the strongest one's weight.
- **The pull:** a song scores up to 0.12 for being of the lanes, by its share in them (all of a song in either lane counts in full). That's enough that most of the mix holds together, but a fine song of another style still gets in.

The description names each lane that at least a fifth of the mix is mostly of, when together they're at least half of it.

### 4. Shape: the order

- **Energy boost** builds and **Wind down** settles. The mix's own songs are sorted by energy and cut into five equal steps. Steps keep their order, and within a step songs are ordered to blend (tempo, key, energy: `blendOrder`).
- **Chill** rises and falls (the "smooth" order genre mixes use).
- **Deep focus** starts from the song in the middle of its energy, and each next song is the one that blends best. Energy is part of blending, so it holds level.

### 5. The words

Energy boost names its tempo range: the middle 80% of the tempos of songs with a sure beat, when at least 60% of them have one. Deep focus says "Instrumental" only when 90% of its songs are very likely without singing (vocals ≤ 0.2).

### 6. Making them again

- **The recipe has a version** (`RECIPE` in `mixes.ts`). Bumping it remakes every account's mixes on the next background check, so a server update brings the new mood mixes at once.
- **A new period leans away:** a mix made for a new hour, day or week, or drawn again with New mix, leans away from the songs of the one it replaces. A mix made again for the same period, because a setting changed, is the same draw as before.
- **Reading the songs:** the mixes' picture of the library is read again once analysis has measured or tagged 100 more songs (or lyrics lookups found 100 more), or fewer once the last read is five minutes old (so a small library, and analysis's last songs, are seen too), not after every song. Reading 50,000 songs takes about a second, yields to requests as it goes, and is shared by everything asking at once.

## How we'll know it works

- **The archetype tests:** Energy boost has old rock and new dance music but no hot-mastered ballad; Chill has lo-fi and bossa but nothing intense or dark; Wind down has ballads, piano and ambient, but no dark ambient, crescendo, applause or interlude; Deep focus has no singing and no crescendo; each builds, rolls, holds or settles as it should.
- **On the household's library:** play each mix through and note every song that doesn't belong. Each one points at a quality or a threshold to tune.
- **Later:** skip rates per mix, once plays say which mix they came from (next).
